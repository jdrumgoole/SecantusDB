Throughput as writers are added, relative to each server's own single-writer rate. mongod reaches 3.4× at eight writers, our default to 2.6× (10.2k docs/s); in absolute terms mongod is 1.5× ahead at one writer and 1.9× at eight. The solid lines are both fully durable.

The dashed line is the opt-in async stack: it starts 1.9× higher than our default, but a hard crash can lose the oplog tail since the last checkpoint; the data itself stays durable.

The benchmarking lesson: over a real network, one server droplet and two client droplets, we reach 0.51× of mongod's throughput. The same build first scored 0.14×, which looked like a huge regression. It wasn't: our server, as a replica-set primary, waits for the journal on every default write, and the benchmark had compared it against a standalone mongod that never waits. Put mongod in the same mode and it loses about half its throughput. Compare like with like.
